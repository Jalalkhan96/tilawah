About these hadith

The pool contains 365 hadith — one for every day of the year — drawn from Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Jami` at-Tirmidhi, Sunan Abu Dawud and Sunan Ibn Majah, with the collection and hadith number shown on every card. Texts are copied exactly from the source editions (fawazahmed0/hadith-api); nothing is paraphrased or generated. The Arabic edition includes the chain of narration (isnad) before the text, as published.

Appearance & reading

All four translations above are bundled with the app and work offline: two English, one Urdu and one Pashto. Twenty-five more verified translations (French, Turkish, Persian, Russian, Chinese, Malay, Indonesian, German, Spanish, Malayalam, Tamil, Bengali, Kurdish, Uzbek, Azerbaijani, Hindi and more Urdu) are on the Translations page — they load per ayah over the internet and are cached on this device.

Audio

23 reciters, every one verified against a real per-ayah audio source before being listed: 17 from the Islamic Network CDN (Alafasy, Husary, Minshawi — Murattal and Mujawwad, Hudhaify, Abdul Basit, Shuraim, Ajamy, Shatri, Jibreel and more) and 6 from EveryAyah (Saad Al-Ghamdi, Ali Abdullah Jaber, Salah Al-Budair, Husary Muallim teaching recitation, Abdul Basit Mujawwad, Aziz Alili). Ayah audio is streamed per-ayah and cached on this device after first play, so listened passages work offline. Word-by-word audio is streamed per word from the Quran.com CDN (audio.qurancdn.com) and cached the same way. Use “⬇ Audio for offline” to pre-download a whole surah's ayah audio. With spoken translation on, each ayah is followed by its English or Urdu translation read aloud, exactly like the pen. Reciters whose per-ayah recordings could not be verified in any free source (for example Sudais and Yasser Al-Dosari) are honestly not listed rather than faked.

About the text, words & the engine

Quranic text: Uthmani script edition served by the AlQuran Cloud API (Tanzil text), validated locally — 114 surahs, 6,236 ayahs, per-surah ayah counts and Juz / page / ruku / hizb metadata checked (see docs/VALIDATION_REPORT.md). Translations (Saheeh International and Muhammad Asad in English, Fateh Muhammad Jalandhry in Urdu, Abdul Wali Khan in Pashto) come from the same API and were merged only after their ayah counts and global numbering matched the validated text exactly, ayah for ayah.

The Basmala is part of the first ayah’s text in this source for most surahs (except 1 and 9), exactly as distributed — the text is preserved unmodified. Word segmentation, word meanings and recitation timings come from the Quran.com API and were machine-verified against the displayed text for 6,233 of 6,236 ayahs; the remaining ayahs honestly fall back to whole-ayah playback. The comparison engine is a local DTW acoustic-alignment engine (); its supported error categories and limits are documented in docs/LIMITATIONS.md.

Word-by-Word Quran

Tap any word to hear exactly that word. Word audio is real per-word recordings (Quran.com CDN) — never text-to-speech. Word meanings are available in English; Urdu and Pashto word-by-word meanings are not in any verified free dataset, so they are not invented here.

Reciters — only verified voices are listed

Every reciter here was checked against a real per-ayah audio source (Islamic Network CDN or EveryAyah) before being listed, and changing reciter really changes the audio file that plays. Famous names whose per-ayah recordings could not be verified in a free, permitted source — including Abdul Rahman Al-Sudais, Yasser Al-Dosari, Abdul Rashid Sufi and Dukali Muhammad Al-Alam — are not shown, rather than being faked with someone else's voice.

Tafsir — sourced commentary, shown with its source

Tafsir text is fetched live from the Quran.com API and needs an internet connection; the source is named on every result. Tafsir Jalalayn is not in the Quran.com API catalog, so it is not offered rather than fabricated.

Tajweed colours — legend

Colours come from the verified quran-tajweed annotation edition (AlQuran Cloud). Annotations were mapped onto this app's validated text only where the two texts could be proven identical ayah by ayah — that proof succeeded for 5,110 of 6,236 ayahs (81.9%); the remaining ayahs honestly render without colours.
